Lunar Occlusion Tracking

A rover mast camera tracks astronauts walking among boulders at the lunar south pole. When someone steps behind a rock or into a long polar shadow, does the tracker keep their identity, or call them a new person when they reappear?

I built a 3-D visibility model (line of sight to the body against half-buried boulders, plus cast shadows under a ~7° sun) and used it inside the tracker: a missed detection counts as evidence of absence only in proportion to the expected detection probability. Trackers then climb a ladder: occlusion awareness, negative information, appearance re-identification, a second camera, and lidar. Each step is measured on identical detections.

A finding I did not expect: adding sensors makes a naive tracker worse (75.6 → 254 switches), because every sensor that cannot see a person reports them as gone. More sensors only help a tracker that knows what each one can see. A second viewpoint helped more than a better sensor at the same spot.

Implemented three times as cross-checks: a browser lab (JavaScript, Three.js, official NASA 3-D models), MATLAB (validated against MathWorks trackerGNN, trackerJPDA, trackerTOMHT), and a PyTorch3D pipeline with rendered camera detections.

Selected projects

Autonomous Driving Stack

A full closed-loop stack in Python: EKF localization, open-vocabulary camera perception, LiDAR and radar fusion into a multi-object tracker, prediction, Frenet lattice planning, LQR/PID control and an independent safety monitor. The stack never sees simulator ground truth.

100 / 100 Monte Carlo runs pass (10 scenarios × 10 seeds, 0 collisions). With occlusion reasoning switched off, the occluded-pedestrian scenario collides in 5 of 10 runs.

Sensor Fusion from Raw Signals

A MATLAB study of AV perception from the signal level up: sensor noise modelling (Allan variance, GNSS error), FMCW radar processing from raw IF samples to range-Doppler maps, CFAR detection and calibrated detections. Algorithms are written from scratch and cross-validated against MathWorks toolboxes.

Navigation Filters under Faults

An interactive lab where a Mars rover, a fighter jet or a spacecraft docking with NASA's Gateway is navigated by dead reckoning, EKF, UKF and a particle filter on the same sensor stream, under injected faults. Filters follow Probabilistic Robotics; every analytic Jacobian is checked numerically.

How I work

  • Controlled comparisons. Methods see identical inputs and differ in one thing, so the result can be attributed to it.
  • Statistics, not screenshots. Results are averaged over many seeds with confidence intervals, and overlapping intervals are reported as such.
  • Reproducible. One command regenerates every table, stamped with the git commit that produced it.
  • Cross-validated. Hand-written algorithms are checked against reference implementations (MathWorks toolboxes, TrackEval).
  • Honest about limits. Each project states what it does not claim, and what failed on the way.
