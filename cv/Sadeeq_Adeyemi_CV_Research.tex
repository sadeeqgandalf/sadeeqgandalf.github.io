\section{Research Experience}
\entry{Graduate research: identity through occlusion in MOTS}{University of Maine}{2026 -- present}
How much identity can a tracker keep while an object is hidden, and what earns it? Studied in simulation, on real driving geometry,
and with Meta's SAM 3 and SAM 3.1 run zero-shot.\par\vspace{1pt}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Benchmarked SAM 3 and SAM 3.1 with the official HOTA evaluation. KITTI-MOTS val, cars: HOTA 82.6 and 74.6. MOTS20 train: 72.6 and 61.5.
  \item Defined an event-level measure from MOT17 visibility labels: SAM 3 kept identity through 94\% of 245 occlusions, SAM 3.1 63\%,
        with both falling for hides of 2 seconds or more.
  \item Showed from the source code and a controlled run that about half of SAM 3.1's deficit comes from its shipped tracking settings
        (car HOTA 74.0 to 78.3 against SAM 3's 82.3), and found a fault that stops it on long, crowded sequences.
  \item Built a Kalman-filter identity layer on SAM's output: it repaired SAM 3.1 (identity through occlusion 63\% to 70\%, McNemar
        p = 0.004) but not SAM 3. Motion outside the tracker says where an object is, not who it is.
  \item Mined 10,871 pedestrian occlusions in 1,256 NVIDIA driving clips with a line-of-sight test; on that geometry an occlusion-aware tracker
        kept 57\% of identities through 1 to 5 second hides, against 2\% for a standard tracker.
  \item Built a simulation lab for controlled occlusion experiments: identity kept rose from 0\% to 89\% as visibility reasoning, negative
        information and appearance re-identification were added; MathWorks JIPDA rose from 0\% to 57\% with the same visibility model.
\end{itemize}
