Research

Occlusion-Aware Multi-Object Tracking for Lunar Surface Operations2026 · independent research

Python · PyTorch3D · JavaScript / Three.js · MATLAB — github.com/sadeeqgandalf/lunar-occlusion-tracking

Can a rover's mast camera keep astronauts' identities when they walk behind boulders or into long south-pole shadows (Artemis EVA scenario)?

  • Visibility model. 3-D line of sight from a 2.2 m mast camera to 16 points on each body against half-buried ellipsoid boulders, plus cast shadows from a low polar sun, giving each track an expected detection probability.
  • Tracker. Constant-velocity EKF per track, χ² gating, Hungarian assignment with an explicit missed-detection option, Bernoulli track existence. Occlusion-aware variants treat a miss as evidence of absence only in proportion to expected visibility; a negative-information update, p(x | miss) ∝ p(x)(1 − PD(x)), keeps hidden estimates inside the blind zone.
  • Results (20 seeds × 300 s, identical detections, Wilson 95% intervals). Identities kept through 1–5 s occlusions: 0% naive → 46% with negative information → 89% with appearance re-ID. ID switches per run: 75.6 → 23.3 with one camera, 6.6 fusing camera, lidar and a second (lander) camera.
  • Sensor-fusion finding. Adding sensors to a tracker without a visibility model raises ID switches 3.4× (75.6 → 254): every sensor that cannot see a person reports them gone. A second viewpoint helped more than a better sensor on the same mast.
  • PyTorch3D rendered-camera pipeline. Built PyTorch3D from source on Apple Silicon (C++20 patch). Rendered the scene from the rover mast and a lander camera; detections come from pixels: visible fraction from silhouettes, range from the depth map, bearing from pixel position, and a colour appearance signature. Static scenery rendered once and per-person windows composited by depth: exact, ~20× faster (0.13 s/frame).
  • Re-identification and PHD. DeepSORT-style appearance likelihoods and long-gap re-ID raise same-ID after hides over 5 s from 8% to 50%; an identical-suit control (28%) bounds what appearance can contribute. A particle PHD filter with visibility-dependent PD maps where hidden people can be.
  • Verification. Python port reproduces the JavaScript engine exactly (cross-check test); MATLAB port compared against MathWorks trackerGNN, trackerJPDA (JIPDA) and trackerTOMHT. Metrics: CLEAR-MOT, IDF1, GOSPA, occlusion survival by duration. Every table regenerates from one script, stamped with its git commit.
