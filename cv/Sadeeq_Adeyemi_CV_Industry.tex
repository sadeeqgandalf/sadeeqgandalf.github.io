\section{Research}
\entry{Tracking Through Occlusion}{graduate research, University of Maine}{2026}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Benchmarked SAM 3 and SAM 3.1 zero-shot with the official HOTA evaluation on an A100. On KITTI-MOTS val, cars scored 82.6 HOTA
        with SAM 3 and 74.6 with SAM 3.1. On MOTS20 train, SAM 3 kept a person's identity through 94\% of 245 real occlusions, SAM 3.1 63\%.
  \item Traced half of SAM 3.1's deficit to its shipped tracking settings by reading Meta's source code; a controlled run raised its car HOTA from 74.0 to 78.3.
  \item Added a Kalman-filter layer that gives a lost ID back when the object reappears: SAM 3.1's identity kept through occlusion rose from 63\% to 70\%.
  \item Mined 10,871 pedestrian occlusions from 1,256 NVIDIA driving clips with a ray-cast line-of-sight test, with lidar projected into the fisheye camera.
  \item On that real geometry, an occlusion-aware tracker kept identity through 1 to 5 second hides in 57\% of 1,600 cases; a standard tracker kept 2\%.
  \item Built a live 3D browser lab for occlusion experiments (46 automated tests), cross-checked against MathWorks GNN, JPDA and TOMHT trackers.
\end{itemize}
